Records identify formal proxy roles, scheduling and promotion. Individual voting claims require individual voting evidence.

## Formal roles

The May 15, 2025 APERS signed minutes identify Jason Brady as the Auditor of State's proxy. He presented the Israel Bonds proposal and moved authorization of the stated $25 million to $50 million range. The motion carried. [Minutes, physical pp. 1 and 4](/documents/records/apers-authorization/).

ATRS records separately identify Brady as Dennis Milligan's designee in the Auditor's board role. These are roles documented for the recorded meetings, not a live directory of every official's current position. [ATRS designee record, packet p. 6 and sign-in p. 1](/documents/records/atrs-auditor-designee/).

## Scheduling and outreach

The April itinerary scheduled eight stops. A DFA participant's message confirms the Hudson–Babbitt meeting; seller-authored correspondence reports Fecher and Treasury-team encounters. Keep these distinctions attached to the account. [Itinerary](/documents/records/capitol-itinerary/) · [Participant confirmation](/documents/records/hudson-babbitt-confirmation/) · [Seller report](/documents/records/seller-reported-meetings/).

## Why the roles matter

These records place the Auditor's office in governance, coordination and promotion. They do not prove that a seller controlled a trustee, that every scheduled meeting occurred or that one named person cast the only dissenting vote.
